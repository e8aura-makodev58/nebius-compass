# Round 48 — independent review of the Nebius deck

Reviewed 2026-10-26 against master `f7fc666`. Review only: no deck, builder or application edits.

**Recommendation:** retain the Investigator-led story, but revise before submission. The strongest additions are the bounded tool workflow and candid measurements. The current draft still lacks a judge-accessible demonstration of its lead feature and misdescribes parts of that workflow.

## Method and limits

Read all 20 slides and speaker notes, the builder, the 14-slide AMD baseline (read-only from the Claude worktree), the supplied rules/brief, demo story, change disclosure, evaluation records and relevant implementation. Rendered both PPTX files with LibreOffice to PDF and inspected every slide on contact sheets, plus full-size architecture and Analytics images. This checks LibreOffice rendering; final PowerPoint/YouTube rendering still needs a pass. Historical provider figures were checked against repository summaries, not independently reproduced with paid calls. No deployment access or live provider request was used. Rules assessment uses the supplied transcription, not legal authentication of the current website.

## Findings, most severe first

1. **High — Slides 7, 17, 19: the lead feature remains inaccessible through the described judge path.** Slide 7 honestly says live capture is pending; slide 17 says guest workplaces cannot use the Investigator. `backend/src/server.mjs` (`createRequestHandler`, demo workplace allowlist) actually returns 403 for reports, investigations, cases and Ask. The missing deployed key is reported in `docs/nebius/10_DEMO_STORY.md`, not independently checked in this review. The supplied rules §4 require a working application as represented, reasonable runtime use of Nebius/NVIDIA, working access and availability through judging. A fallback-only public walkthrough cannot demonstrate the promised live tool loop. **Fix:** main/owner must decide a bounded judge-access path, provision the key privately, run the authorized live check, and replace the pending card with an actual cited recommendation and approval capture. Do not remove the blocker card merely to make the draft look finished. The final access instructions need the working URL, private credential delivery and availability plan through December 15; the closing slide should link the repository and public video. Slides 1/20 currently name those deliverables without giving a usable destination.

2. **High — Slide 5: its single flow bypasses the Investigator recommendation and approval gate.** It goes from Ultra interpretation → symptom confirmation → responder ranking → automatic offer → fix ranking. For a confirmed field report, `backend/src/investigation-service.mjs` creates an `INVESTIGATION`-gated issue, runs the tool investigation, and authorizes its chosen fix/responder through `authorizeInvestigation`; `backend/src/dispatch-gate.mjs` rejects an unauthorized initial assignment. Slide 7 correctly describes a later approval, but slide 5 never shows it. This also differs from `10_DEMO_STORY.md` beats 3–5. **Fix:** draw two explicitly labelled paths: ordinary autonomous dispatch and field-report investigation. In the latter show Super interpretation → operator confirmation → Ultra tools/recommendation → code checks/prerequisites → approval → assignment. Keep shared observed-history feedback and the separate overtime decision branch.

3. **High — Slides 3, 5, 7 (and their notes): interpretation ranking is attributed to the wrong model.** `backend/src/investigator.mjs:rankInterpretations` uses role `compass`; `backend/src/model-provider.mjs:NEBIUS_ROLE_DEFAULTS` makes that Super, thinking off. Ultra is the subsequent `investigator` tool-loop role. `docs/evaluation.md` explicitly records Super interpretation ranking. **Fix:** relabel interpretation boxes and narration Super; reserve Ultra for the bounded investigation. Add interpretation ranking to slide 16's Super role row. Check the demo story too, since its prose repeats the same conflation.

4. **High — Slide 11: selecting run B omits the available same-code repeat and hides instability.** Its 46/60, 46/60, 41/60 and discordant counts 7/2 and 2/2 faithfully quote run B. However, `docs/evaluation.md`, “Paired first-fix replay, live,” also reports run C: 45/60, 46/60, 41/60; 23/60 fallbacks versus B's 7/60; discordants 6/2 and 2/3. The slide discusses the older run A but not this less favorable repeat. Saying “the investigator ties” without “in run B” overstates repeatability. **Fix:** show B and C together with fallback counts and the shared window. Say no demonstrated gain over deterministic order; the class-frequency comparison remains descriptive and synthetic. Keep the no-significance caveat. Do not describe run A's higher count as a model-quality improvement.

5. **High — Slides 1, 19, 20: the required significant-update disclosure is absent.** `HACKATHON_CHANGES.md` discloses an AMD submission, 119 commits before baseline `1e1c69c`, and specific Nebius-era additions. The supplied rules §4 explicitly require a written explanation for existing projects, and §6 rejects a superficial rebrand. Merely changing provider names or adding a roadmap does not communicate this. **Fix:** include a compact “existing foundation → Nebius additions” panel: prior dispatch/simulation/wall product; new field reports, bounded Ultra tools, public certifications/safety gates, Nebius role routing, evaluation and usage accounting. Cite the baseline and disclosure document; put the complete explanation on the submission form/README. The deck alone is not the whole submission, so this is missing evidence, not a finding of ineligibility.

6. **Medium — Slides 7, 8, 13: absolute human-approval and safety promises exceed the code.** Slide 7 says an assignment is accepted “only after a person approves,” while `finishInvestigationDeadline` supports opt-in `AUTO_APPROVE_SAFE`, and `assertDispatchAuthorized` accepts `AUTO_APPROVED`. Slide 13's notes acknowledge staffing auto-approval while its face says nothing changes without central-command approval. Slide 8's “Instructions ... are not followed” sounds like a universal injection guarantee; `report-policy.mjs` uses a bounded lexicon, and validators are not an exhaustive semantic proof. **Fix:** state “operator approval by default; explicitly enabled, audited policy may approve eligible safe cases.” Describe untrusted-text handling and rejection/repair as implemented controls; keep the finite scenario counts without presenting them as universal safety coverage. Put the policy qualification on the visible slide as well as in notes.

7. **Medium — Slide 15: the reopen denominator is untraceable to the saved capture, and its period is ambiguous.** `assets/screen-analytics.png` shows 25% with **n=4,682**, not 4,683. Its reopen KPI sits above the period picker; `backend/src/analytics-service.mjs:issueAnalytics` computes it over snapshot-wide observed attempts. The September Pareto bars are 1,280 + 1,059 + 665 = **3,004**, and the top two are 2,339/3,004 = **77.86%**, so 78% and crossing 80% on class three are supported. That does not make the reopen KPI a September statistic. `10_DEMO_STORY.md` repeats 4,683 without an immutable corresponding capture. **Fix:** use one frozen export/capture, record its snapshot and numerator/denominator, and label September Pareto versus full-history reopen rate separately. If 4,683 is a later snapshot, retain and cite that evidence rather than silently changing one number. Label the percentage as reopened attempts, not reopened issues.

8. **Medium — Slides 4, 7, 13, 15: the requested AMD-content floor is not fully retained.** The baseline's slides 7–8 show a concrete responder comparison and actual first fix: 26 versus 110 similar cases at the same trip time, 87% observed success, a 7.0-minute first fix, agreement/disagreement with baselines and capture-specific validity caveats. Its staffing slide shows a real approval card; its results slide shows resolved/raised counts and weekly first-time-fix behavior. The new deck removes the responder/fix examples, replaces staffing proof with prose, and replaces the monthly time series with different KPIs. **Fix:** restore the useful evidence/story functions, ideally with fresh Nebius captures and frozen current analytics. If retaining AMD evidence, label it explicitly historical AMD output; never relabel it as Nebius. Preserve a compact weekly trend or explain to the owner why that baseline content is being replaced. Do not blindly restore unsupported old monthly figures: the demo story already says the old 73.5% does not appear on today's screen.

9. **Medium — Slide 6: attached connectors do not make a complete or correct architecture graph.** All **31** native connectors in the deck have both start and end shape references (slides 3:3, 5:11, 6:13, 7:4); there is no unglued-arrow defect. But slide 6's fallback planner has only an input and no route to validation/state; Baselines is isolated; Compass has an input but no downstream dispatch edge; Responders → Dispatch reverses the offer/response story without a label. Narrator → Central command is unexplained. The builder's architecture `link(...)` calls confirm the omissions. **Fix:** draw the application-owned data path from context to bounded models/baselines to validation/gate to dispatch, and outcomes back to state. Label feedback versus command arrows; connect fallback proposals to the same validator and narratives to their checked record. Restore the AMD diagram's color legend. Also qualify “Models only rank”: Lightning proposes events and drafts narrative, as the slide notes correctly say.

10. **Medium — Slide 3: the no-history refusal statement is too strong.** The footer says a report matching no equipment **or history** is refused. `backend/src/report-policy.mjs:interpretationOptions` builds up to 12 public symptom candidates for matched equipment without checking case history; no-history evidence can instead yield abstention later in `deterministicInvestigation`. **Fix:** distinguish equipment resolution from evidence sufficiency: unmatched equipment stays unconfirmed; unsupported investigation recommendations abstain. Do not imply that interpretation candidates establish a factual diagnosis or hidden fault cause.

11. **Medium — Slides 3–8, 10, 16, 18: decorative art competes with content, and evidence is too small for a video.** In the render, light gray subtitles cross bright equipment/icons (especially slides 3, 4, 7, 10); slide 5's explanatory text crosses the compass artwork. Six thumbnails on slide 4 show product breadth but cannot expose the cited evidence or approval record at a three-minute-video viewing size. Slide 16's small pricing table and slide 18's console need a close-up to be meaningful proof. **Fix:** use the existing plain background for dense content/diagrams, keep artwork mainly on cover/close, enlarge the key evidence screenshot, and move secondary pricing/probe detail into an appendix. No obvious off-slide shape, text-box overflow or card-to-card collision was found in this render. Numbering is consistent for slides 2–19; intentionally unnumbered cover/close is acceptable, though the tiny gray footer numbers need more contrast.

12. **Low — Slides 10, 12, 16, 18: tighten measurement provenance and denominator labels.** Slide 10 accurately copies the small generic ranking probe, but the real Compass packet table in `05_TOKEN_FACTORY_FACTS.md` is stronger evidence for default role choices (Super/off 5/5 valid, 3.2 s; Lightning/off planner 5/5, 1.3 s). Slide 12's accepted/repaired/deterministic/raw-invalid values are overlapping categories; viewers may add 12+2+11+2 and infer an inconsistent total. Its “8/8 abstention precision and recall” also merges two separate ratios. Pricing is dated only in slide 16's notes, and the four-role presentation omits the configured Super `assistant` role. **Fix:** cite the dated source visibly; distinguish generic probe from application packet measurements, explain overlap and denominators, show precision 8/8 and recall 8/8 separately, and date prices on-slide. Include or explicitly scope out Ask Compass. Keep the accepted-citation caveat: returned-reference validity is not semantic grounding. Slide 18's 635 hermetic tests is verified below; 208 is the browser suite's discovered count, with completion reported separately below.
